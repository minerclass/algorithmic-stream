The Algorithmic Stream

Sources drift past. The current gets faster with every reach. Net what looks useful, verify it before you cite it, and see what survives the audit.

Your research question

What happened on the night the levee broke at Miller's Crossing in March 1871, and how do we know?

Miller's Crossing is invented. Every source in this river is an illustrative composite, so nothing here can be cited for real. The lateral-reading moves are the real part.

How to play, and keyboard controls
  1. Net a source. Click or tap a card drifting down the river, or pick it from the list under the river. It lands in your dock.
  2. Read laterally. Each docked source offers three moves: Who is behind it?, What is the evidence?, and What do other sources say? Each move reveals a clue. In a timed run, each costs twelve seconds.
  3. Decide. Cite it or let it go. You need five citations. The audit at the end tells you what held.
  4. Raise the dam when the river is too fast to read. Hold the dam button or the space bar. It drains while held and refills while released.
  5. Resist the autopilot. A button offers to fill your bibliography instantly. It is allowed. It is also how the stream chooses for you.

        Dissertation bridge

        Public scholarship connected to Micah Miner's dissertation research on pedagogical friction in the age of generative AI. Use the run as a prompt for thinking alongside the current study, not as a finding from it. The three prompts below are the game's, not the study's research questions.

        • Educator sensemaking. Which moves in this run restored productive friction, and which felt like delay? Where would a student feel the difference?
        • Infrastructural conditions. The deadline and the autopilot are the conditions. Which conditions in a school reward the stream's choice over the reader's?
        • Framework-aligned supports. What assessment language would make the three lateral-reading moves an expected part of AI-assisted research rather than an optional one?
